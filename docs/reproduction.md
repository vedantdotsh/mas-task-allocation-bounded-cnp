## Main finding

The recovered June implementation and the later paper are numerically inconsistent. The four recovered mechanisms reproduce all 30 saved trials' original metrics within numerical tolerance (`tests/test_recovered_base.py`), including loads, token proxy, and latency. The discrepancy with Table 1 therefore predates this extension; changing parameters to erase it would break the recovered implementation. SSI and CBBA are reconstructions, not recovered implementations.

CNP makespan is 613.94 versus greedy 622.37 (-1.35%). Message saving is 54.17%. The paired CNP-minus-greedy makespan difference is -8.43, with bootstrap 95% CI [-16.77, 0.38]. This interval does not establish equivalence or a 1% noninferiority bound.

## Diagnosis, without parameter fitting

- The expected qualitative message ordering holds: CBBA has the most messages; CNP saves exactly 13/24 = 54.1667% versus greedy when n=12 and k=5. This saving is an accounting identity, not a statistical discovery.
- Nearest-agent remains clearly worse than CNP. CNP and greedy are close, but their mean makespans differ by more than 1% in this recovered configuration. The later paper's much shorter waits and smaller makespans are not reproduced. It omits the speed distribution; the recovered source fixes it at U(0.8,1.3). There is insufficient evidence to attribute the later values to a particular changed parameter or code defect.
- Both wait definitions are saved. Changing service wait to queue-only wait does not justify silently replacing the original definition. All tasks drain after the arrival horizon; throughput is T/makespan.
- The paper's token assumptions differ from June's. `paper_tokens` applies the later prices to the same observed ledger, while `proxy_tokens` and `estimated_token_cost` preserve June's model. This isolates pricing changes from allocation changes.
- This synchronous append-only CBBA is close to SSI in allocation quality. Its message magnitude is close to Table 1, but neither matching magnitude nor the algorithm's name recovers the lost variant. Claiming CBBA is inherently worse in quality is not supported by this implementation.

The later paper's re-bidding improvement is also not reproduced: at noise σ=0.5, single-round makespan is 841.07 and one-re-bid makespan is 868.52, with 157.15 extra rounds per trial. The recovered policy excludes an underquoted winner, which can exclude a truly good agent; re-bidding is not guaranteed to improve allocation. The paper experiment's explicit 1.25 threshold is used; June's compatibility default remains 1.35. No threshold or randomness was tuned.

At four agents and k=5 capped at n, clean CNP and greedy have identical assignments and gaps. The paper's unequal gaps cannot follow from that setup. The exact solver permits task reordering and immediate release-time decisions; batch methods wait until window close, so their gap includes that policy restriction.

For CNP in Experiment 1, mean last arrival is 497.41, mean drain time is 116.53, mean queue-only wait is 36.76, and mean response time is 69.41. These diagnostics expose arrival-horizon effects.
